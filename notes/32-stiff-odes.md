# 32 Stiff ODEs

`calccode/integrators.py` grows two solvers. `rk45_adaptive` is Dormand-Prince: seven evaluations of f per step produce both a 5th order and a 4th order update, and their difference, scaled by tol * (1 + |y|), drives an accept/reject loop that grows the step by up to 5x or shrinks it to a fifth. On exponential decay at tol = 1e-9 it reaches t = 5 in 54 steps with error 2e-10. Fixed RK4 needs more than 100 steps to get under that error on the same problem. On the oscillator over 20 time units it lands on cos(t) to 3e-9 in 311 steps. Adaptivity pays when the solution has calm stretches.

Then van der Pol at mu = 1000 ruins it. Over t in [0, 100] at a loose tol = 1e-4, `rk45_adaptive` takes 86605 accepted steps. The fast transient is dead after a fraction of a time unit, so accuracy would allow a large step. Stability does not. The Jacobian has a mode near -3000 on the slow manifold, and any explicit step past about 1e-3 excites it, so the controller sits at the stability boundary and crawls. `backward_euler` covers the same span in 2000 steps of h = 0.05, stays bounded at |y| <= 2, and finishes in a fifth of a second where the explicit run needs seven. That contrast is the definition of stiffness: the step limit comes from a mode that is not even in the answer.

Where this breaks: implicit methods need a Jacobian and Newton per step, and the cost per step is real. Each step solves z = y_n + h f(t, z) by Newton, each Newton iteration factors I - h J with a hand-written Gaussian elimination, and with no analytic Jacobian the finite-difference fallback costs 2n extra f evaluations per iteration. Backward Euler is also only first order, and its unconditional stability cuts both ways: it damps the stiff mode you want gone and the oscillations you wanted to keep. A production stiff solver would be BDF or Radau with a reused factored Jacobian; this module keeps one method per idea.
