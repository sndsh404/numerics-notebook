# 27 Polar and Parametric Arc Length

Note 20 ended with the observation that the circle's arc length singularity was never in the curve; it was in the choice of x as the parameter. This note is the follow-through. `calccode/applications.py` now has `arc_length_parametric(x, y, a, b)`, integrating sqrt((dx/dt)^2 + (dy/dt)^2), and `arc_length_polar(r, a, b)`, integrating sqrt(r^2 + (dr/dtheta)^2). The polar formula is the parametric one with x = r cos(theta), y = r sin(theta) substituted and simplified; the product rule terms cross-multiply, the cross terms cancel, and two trig identities collapse what is left.

Both functions are the same shape as everything else in the module: build the integrand, differentiate with `central_diff`, integrate with `simpson`. The polar circle r = const is a free test, since dr/dtheta vanishes and the integrand is the constant r. The real test is the cardioid r = 1 + cos(theta). Its arc length integrand simplifies by hand to 2 |cos(theta/2)|, which integrates to exactly 8 over [0, 2 pi]. A closed-form answer that clean is a gift: the numerical result comes back within 1e-6, and the only reason it is not machine precision is the cusp.

Where this breaks: that cusp. At theta = pi the cardioid pinches to the origin, r = 0 and dr/dtheta = 0, so the integrand hits zero with a |cos| corner. Simpson assumes smoothness and the cusp costs it; the error decays slower than the usual h^4 near the pinch and I needed n = 4000 where a smooth curve converges at n = 200. The other trap is theta range: r(theta) traces the full cardioid once on [0, 2 pi], but polar curves in general retrace themselves. A rose r = cos(3 theta) traced over [0, 2 pi] draws every petal twice, and the integral happily reports twice the true length. The code integrates what you give it; it has no idea whether the curve revisited a point.
