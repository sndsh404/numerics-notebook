# 30 Vector Calculus

`calccode/vector_calculus.py` is the Calc III core, and it is mostly recombination. A double integral is Simpson inside Simpson, with the inner bounds allowed to be functions of x so triangles and disks work directly. A line integral adds one central difference for the tangent vector. A surface integral adds two, one per parameter, and the magnitude of their hand-written cross product gives the area element. Nothing new had to be invented; the earlier modules carry the weight.

The theorem checkers are the part I care about. Green, divergence, and Stokes each compute both sides of the identity independently and return both numbers. The line side of Green's theorem on the unit disk comes back at 1e-10, because the integrand along the circle is constant and Simpson eats it. The area side is the same 2 pi but only to 3e-4 at n = 400. The divergence theorem on the unit cube is exact to roundoff on both sides, since the field (x, y, z) is linear and Simpson never misses a linear function. Stokes on the flat disk inherits Green's split: circulation exact, flux of the curl coarse.

`lagrange_critical` stacks grad f = lambda grad g on top of g = 0 and runs Newton on the combined system, with the jacobian from multivar and each solve from linalg. From (1, 0.5) it lands on x = y = 1/sqrt(2) in a handful of steps, and from (-1, -0.3) it finds the other branch. One start gives one root, so a problem with several extrema needs several calls.

Where this breaks: curved boundaries, not curved integrands. The disk tests pass y bounds of sqrt(1 - x^2) into the inner integral, and the derivative of that boundary blows up at the endpoints of the outer integral. The singularity drags Simpson from its usual n^-4 down to about n^-1.5, which I measured directly: halving the error takes roughly four times the panels. That is why the Green and Stokes tests run n = 400 with a 5e-4 tolerance instead of the 1e-9 the rest of the module earns. The honest fixes are a tanh-style substitution that flattens the boundary or a polar change of variables, and both beat refinement.
